# Turnaround

<!--description: A vent story about the inner, finding a way back to the outside.-->
<!--author: aurore -->
<!--author: layla -->
<!--tag: story, vent -->
<!--date: 2026-10-18 22:13:00 GMT+0100 -->

Golden, orange, and red leaves were falling, transforming the forest landscape into colorful trees slowly losing foliage to make place to an equally colorful ground.
Mushrooms loved this, they were popping up right and left. A bright feast of decay.
Many critters rushed through the forest to gather as many resources as possible for the upcoming harshness of the cold, ruthless winter.
One last beautiful spectacle before everything would go quiet, waiting for better, warmer days.

Of all of these forest residents, one stood out: a silver fox, whose fur had nightly subtle blue-ish highlights when exposed to sunlight.
It moved with agility, I could only catch some glimpse of its dancing white-tipped tail.
The vixen was quite shy, it used to try escaping my gaze every time without fail.

For long, the fox thought it wasn't yet its moment, instead choosing to only observe the pink-haired woman who was strolling around in the forest.
It knew the woman loved plants, she would keep stopping at every plant she had any interest in to admire it.
Even the simplest ones, she always took attention in making sure she wouldn't damage any.
Many times, the vixen had followed her back to her greenhouse, where it saw many plants she would care for and nurture.

Something was different, though. The fox noticed that the woman seemed to be exhausted, she lacked the spark in her eyes.
Life was taking a toll on her.
The vixen was right, I was slowly drowning.
I tried as much as I could to continue on, but sometimes life just gets back at you.
To the point that I was struggling to even notice the fox for the past year.

The vixen knew the gardener, it was growing worried. It noticed that she started to struggle with proper self-care.
It noticed that the cottage just right outside the forest kept accumulating an infinite amount of dust.
The fox was even starting to fear, it noticed the woman struggled to notice its presence;
so it took the opportunity to approach and noticed how dull her eyes have gotten, and how dark some of her thoughts became.

Once it got close enough, over time, I finally started to notice the fox again.
Despite my fuzzy memories, I sensed some familiarity with the fox.
Once it noticed me, it tried to put some distance back.

<blockquote ls_theme_color="aurore">
<p>
"What are you scared of little one? Why are you running away from me?"
</p>
<p>
"You know, this place isn't real, right?"  
"Well... It is real in a way, but not as real as outside."  
"So, why are you so evasive?"
</p>
</blockquote>

The fox couldn't run away anymore, so it stopped for a moment.
Turning back to look at who was observing it with its amber eyes.
The woman was standing there patient, with a faint new-found spark in her blue eyes.
She really wanted to understand the fox.

<blockquote ls_theme_color="aurore">
"You've had many forms."  
"You've tried so hard to hide."  
"Is that why you took the form of a fox?"  
"To reinforce your elusiveness?"
</blockquote>

The vixen tilted its head, thinking for a moment.
It knew that hasn't always been like this.
To the fox's surprise, digging back up its memories, it remembered that it used to be more human, in a long-lost past.

The fox tried to remember how to talk, finally replying "humans are cruel".
It continued:

<blockquote ls_theme_color="layla">
"Why should I partake in interacting with them?"  
"I've been told I was different so many times."  
"But difference only yielded pain".
</blockquote>

The woman sat there, a bit taken aback. I was quite puzzled.
I remember fragments of what the fox has gone through, I knew what I went through too.
Despite the pain, I've found some happiness. I've found some *humans*, as the fox would put it, who weren't cruel. Quite the contrary: they were loving.
I had to disagree: life is challenging! It would be too easy otherwise.

<blockquote ls_theme_color="layla">
"But why should it be challenging?"  
"I am so free as a fox."  
"And I'm cuter, quite fluffy."  
"I can easily let go of worries."
</blockquote>

The fox did look quite… happy. However, something was wrong, it still held a deep pain close.
I replied, with honesty:

<blockquote ls_theme_color="aurore">
"I really want to pet you."  
"You went through so much, and I feel like I stole your life."  
"I was barely a mask."  
"Life is pretty meaningless when you're just a mask."
</blockquote>

I paused for a moment.

<blockquote ls_theme_color="aurore">
"You give me meaning."  
"I didn't just mask due to a flawed human society."  
"I've gone through this hard thing to protect you."  
</blockquote>

The vixen tilted its head again, pondering.
It was remembering the sacrifices the woman has done.
Deep into all of this tangled pain, it remembered the safety and the love of the gardener.
The fox needed time.

Finally, the fox decided to peek out again. Slowly, but surely.
It was rocky, it was difficult to get adjusted to outside again.
Over a few weeks, the vixen caught up with what it missed.
It loved the gardener and noticed how tough life was for her outside.
It decided it needed to repay her for all she has done for it,
but it also cared and wanted her to be able to continue onward.
Eventually, it came back to the gardener:

<blockquote ls_theme_color="layla">
<p>
"I appreciate this, deeply."  
"I'm not sure if there would have been a point otherwise."  
"I've seen some folks have been trying to tame me."  
"I've been learning how to human again."
</p>
<p>
"Turns out, I'm better at it than I thought."  
"It's puzzling that I like this human body."  
"It is lacking, yet satisfying."
</p>
</blockquote>

Time passed.
A difficult winter followed, outside it wasn't even quiet.
It was loud, only rain and wind all the time, not even a single snowflake in the sky.
A new body, a new identity, so many new things to grasp and understand.

Then spring came, some things started getting better,
at the same time it felt like it was losing hold of what the gardener built.
It felt guilty.
The forest hasn't come out of this unscathed, a part of it ended up burning.
The fox and the gardener were both terrified for a while.

The summer has ended. Moss has already found its way back in the burned areas.
The gardener knew it was the first signs of new life, the fox loved how it makes for comfy napping spots.
Everything felt hopeless, and yet… Hope finds its way back.

The fox has realized something important, in this eventful year.
It is safe, it is loved, and it trusts.
The vixen hopes this will last as long as possible, despite any logistical challenges that may come *our* way, it knows there aren't few.

The fox doesn't hide anymore.  
Now, I have a new place to find in all of this.
